# English structured abstract

**Title:** Incremental value of laboratory information and model complexity for identifying prevalent moderate-to-severe periodontitis: a survey-weighted cross-cycle NHANES study

**Objective:** To assess the incremental value of laboratory information and model complexity for identifying prevalent moderate-to-severe periodontitis, using questionnaire information as a reference.

**Methods:** This exploratory analysis used NHANES 2013–2014 for development (n=3,617) and 2011–2012 for earlier-cycle validation (n=3,323). CDC/AAP 2012 surveillance definitions were applied to interproximal measurements. Five information panels were compared using age-spline ridge logistic regression, additive explainable boosting machines (EBM) and XGBoost under survey-weighted, PSU-grouped nested cross-validation. Laboratory increments compared 47-variable with 11-variable non-laboratory/blood-pressure panels within algorithms; algorithm increments were assessed within panels. Paired design intervals used 1,000 conditional resamples; 200 additional resamples repeated development for the logistic laboratory comparison. SHAP, EBM curves and deletion/refitting analyses examined information use and substitutability.

**Results:** Questionnaire logistic regression achieved validation AUC 0.801 (conditional 95% interval 0.785–0.816), compared with 0.800 and 0.801 for the 11- and 47-variable panels. Laboratory AUC increments were 0.0009, −0.0002 and 0.0066 for logistic regression, EBM and XGBoost, respectively; all conditional intervals included zero. The full-procedure logistic interval was −0.0168 to 0.0084. Questionnaire EBM and XGBoost achieved AUCs of 0.795 and 0.792. Deletion/refitting supported age and smoking information and suggested partial substitutability between self-reported smoking and cotinine. Questionnaire logistic calibration intercept/slope were 0.404/1.051; AUC was 0.646 among adults aged ≥65 years.

**Conclusions:** Additional laboratory information and model complexity did not establish consistent incremental performance in these observable NHANES domains. Attribution strength did not necessarily indicate irreplaceable predictive benefit. Probability miscalibration and limited discrimination among older adults constrain direct application; findings do not establish equivalence, causation or clinical utility.
